# 16 — KIADB-Zone Land Banking, Mapping and Investor Leads

This file adds two lead domains to the engine in file 15:

- **Domain 1 — Land Bank:** systematic mapping of land parcels around KIADB industrial areas and Bengaluru's high-growth belts.
- **Domain 2 — Investor Bank:** people with capital and/or idle land who want a warehouse as an income asset and need a team like us to build it.

Together with **tenant demand** (file 15 §3), these form our **three-sided matchmaking engine**:

```
   LAND BANK                INVESTOR BANK               TENANT DEMAND
 (owners, parcels)     (capital, idle land, HNIs)    (3PLs, e-com, mfg)
          \                      |                        /
           \                     |                       /
            +------->  STHIRA: match, develop, lease  <--+
                                 |
                       Leased, financed warehouse
                                 |
                    Exit / hold (LRD, SM REIT, funds)
```

**Our edge:** most players have only one of the three. A broker has tenants, a landowner has land, an HNI has money. **We keep all three pipelines full and connect them.**

## PART A — Why KIADB zones, and which land to target

### A1. Three kinds of land around a KIADB area (target the right one)

| Type | What it is | Our approach |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Private land just outside a KIADB boundary (0–5 km ring)** ⭐ | Agricultural or converted land owned by families, next to industrial roads, power and workforce | **Main target.** Tenants (suppliers, 3PLs serving the factories) want to be close. Conversion is easier where the Master Plan already shows industrial use |
| **2. Allotted KIADB plots lying idle** | Industrial plots allotted to companies or individuals but not built on | **Investor-type lead** (Part B). Allottees face forfeiture risk if the plot isn't used within the allotted period ([DH](https://www.deccanherald.com/content/436883/govt-bans-use-kiadb-land.html)). Warehousing or sub-letting needs **KIADB consent** under the lease-cum-sale terms. Make "KIADB consent possible?" a qualification check |
| **3. Land notified for KIADB acquisition** | Land under a preliminary/final acquisition notification | **❌ Avoid.** It can be frozen, contested or acquired ([buyer guide](https://www.propnewz.com/blog/kiadb-land-acquisition-north-bengaluru-buyer-due-diligence-2026-06-09)). *But* owners just **outside** the notified boundary, or whose land was **dropped** from acquisition, are excellent leads: they've seen the value and want to unlock it |

> **Rule:** every parcel is checked against KIADB acquisition notifications (Karnataka Gazette / kiadb.in) **before** the first owner meeting. One mistake here destroys trust.

## PART B — Building the Land Bank: the mapping system

### B1. Free tools (all government or free)
| Tool | Use |
|---|---|
| **kiadb.in** | Industrial-area list, layout maps, allotment notices, acquisition notifications |
| **Karnataka Gazette** (egazette) | Official acquisition notifications under the KIAD Act, used to **exclude** notified land |
| **Dishank app** | Survey-number boundaries on satellite imagery |
| **Bhoomi RTC** (landrecords.karnataka.gov.in) | Owner names, extent, land type, mutation history |
| **Kaveri 2.0** | Registered sale/lease transactions nearby, showing price trends and who's buying |
| **Local Planning Authority Master Plans** (Hoskote, Nelamangala, Doddaballapur, BIAAPA for Devanahalli, BMRDA) | Zoning: industrial / logistics / agricultural / green belt |
| **Google Earth Pro** (free) | **Historical imagery**: see which areas changed in 2–3 years (new sheds = demand moving in) |
| **Google My Maps** (free, shareable) | **The Land Bank Map** that the whole team uses on their phones |

### B2. Land Bank Map: layers
1. KIADB industrial area boundaries (traced from KIADB layout maps)
2. ❌ Notified acquisition areas (red, do not approach)
3. Highways, STRR interchanges, MMLP, airport
4. Existing warehouses and factories (potential tenants, plus proof of demand)
5. **Target parcels** coloured by score: 🟢 A / 🟡 B / ⚪ C
6. Parcel status: Not contacted / Owner reached / Snapshot sent / Visit / Mandate / Not interested

### B3. The 6-step mapping routine (per zone)
1. **Draw a 5 km ring** around the KIADB boundary or STRR interchange.
2. **Spot parcels ≥3 acres** that are undeveloped, in one block, with road frontage (Dishank + Google Earth). ~2 hours per zone.
3. **Pull RTC** for each survey number: owner names, extent, land type. Note the number of co-owners.
4. **Exclusion check:** Gazette/KIADB notification? Green belt? Tank bed? Litigation? → mark red.
5. **Score the parcel (out of 10):** distance to KIADB gate/interchange ≤2 km (2) · road ≥12 m (2) · 4–15 acres (2) · industrial zoning or easy conversion (2) · ≤4 owners on RTC (1) · visible demand nearby, e.g., new sheds in Google Earth (1).
6. **Reach the owner warmly**, through a local document writer, broker, panchayat member or neighbour (file 15, Source 4).

### B4. Zone Captains and targets
- Give each corridor one **Zone Captain** (initially the field BD for East + North; add a second field BD for West + North by month 4).
- **Targets per Zone Captain:** 25 new parcels mapped per week · 10 owners reached per week · 3 A-parcels per month in the pipeline.
- **Land Bank size targets:** 150 parcels by month 3 · 400 by month 6 · 800+ by month 12. *A large, clean, scored land bank is a company asset in itself.* Tenants, brokers, investors and institutional buyers will all want access to it.

| Channel | How it targets KIADB zones |

### C2. Why capital investors should NOT buy land (our key investor insight)

**Buying land kills the return. Leasing land from an owner makes it work.**

| | Investor buys the land | Investor uses our land-lease model (Hoskote-style) |
|---|---|---|
| Land (≈4.7 acres, Hoskote-type values) | ~₹40–50 Cr | ₹0 (landowner gets 33% of rent) |
| Building (1.13 L sq ft) | ~₹13 Cr | ~₹13 Cr |
| Rent at ₹23.5/sq ft | ~₹3.2 Cr/yr | ~₹3.2 Cr/yr × 67% = ~₹2.1 Cr/yr |
| **Gross yield on investment** | **~5.5–6%** | **~16%** (before opex, tax, debt) |

*Illustrative only: replace with real numbers from the rebuilt Hoskote model. The point holds at any realistic numbers: for an investor, a long land lease beats buying land.*

**Investor pitch in one line:** *"We find the land, the tenant and the builders. You fund only the building and earn rent on a cost that excludes land."*

### C3. ⚠️ Compliance: how to market to investors safely
- **Do not advertise "returns" or pool money from many people into one scheme.** That can be treated as a **Collective Investment Scheme** under SEBI rules. Fractional and pooled ownership is regulated (SM REITs exist precisely for this).
- **Do advertise our development service:** *"We develop leased warehouses for investors and landowners."*
- Each project uses **one SPV per project** (already our rule) with one investor or a small, private group. Private-placement rules apply to companies: no public offers, limits on the number of offerees. **Your CA and lawyer must structure every investor SPV.**
- In ads and posts, show **case-study numbers clearly labelled as one project's results**, never "guaranteed X% return".

### C5. Investor funnel
`Content/ads/briefing → 1:1 call (qualify) → Hoskote site visit → Investment Memo for a specific matched site → land-lease LOI with the owner + SPV set-up → development mandate`

**Investor qualification (score out of 10):** investable capital ≥₹5 Cr (3) · 10+ year horizon (2) · comfortable with a land lease, not land ownership (2) · decision-maker (1) · has a CA/lawyer to move quickly (1) · timeline ≤12 months (1).

**What the Investment Memo contains** (5–8 pages): matched site from the Land Bank, land-lease terms proposed to the owner, building spec and cost (3 tiers), tenant demand evidence / LOI status, rent and cash flow (base / downside), financing (LRD), exit options (hold vs sell to SM REIT/fund at a lower yield), our fees, risks.
