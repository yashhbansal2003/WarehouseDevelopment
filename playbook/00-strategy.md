### What these numbers mean for us (plain English)

1. **Demand is real and growing.** Big names are signing leases on both of our corridors right now.
2. **Supply is growing faster.** Savills shows 5.2 msf completed against 3.6 msf absorbed. A landowner who builds a plain shed *and then* looks for a tenant can sit empty for 6–12 months. **That fear is our biggest selling point.**
3. **Rent in Hoskote is ~₹22–24, not ₹26.** DHL, a top-tier tenant, got ₹23.50 plus 3 months free. Amazon's ₹26.25 is on the west corridor, on a 9-year lease. **All landowner projections must use a ₹21–23 base case**, with ₹26 only as the upside.
4. **The west corridor is about to get ~5 msf of government-backed supply (the MMLP).** Pitch Nelamangala–Dabaspete landowners on *built-to-suit for a named tenant*, never on spec.

> **Hoskote 001 numbers check (do this before publishing any "real numbers" post).** At ₹26 × 1,13,000 sq ft, gross rent is ~₹3.53 Cr/yr, so our 67% share is ~₹2.36 Cr. Take away LRD interest on ~₹13 Cr (~₹1.2 Cr at ~9.5%), depreciation, insurance and property tax, and Year-1 profit before tax comes to roughly **₹0.5–0.8 Cr, not ₹3 Cr**. ₹3 Cr PAT only works if there is no debt, the rent has escalated for 10+ years, or the 67/33 split applies to something other than gross rent. At ₹23.50 rent, everything is about 10% lower again. **Rebuild the Hoskote model with actual signed numbers before it becomes our case study.** One wrong number in public costs more trust than ten right ones earn.

## 2. Who we are NOT, and who we compete with

| Competitor type | Examples | Why a landowner might choose them | Why they should choose us |
|---|---|---|---|
| Institutional parks | ESR, IndoSpace, Welspun One, Horizon (Blackstone), LOGOS, Greenbase | They will *buy* the land outright | They want 25–100+ acres. A 3–15 acre family usually can't sell to them and doesn't want to sell |
| JDA developers / local builders | Local promoters on the corridors | They put in their own money | They take 40–60% of the asset forever, often hide costs and pick cheap contractors. Our client keeps the asset |
| PEB companies doing "turnkey" | Kirby, Tata BlueScope, local PEB shops | One vendor for the building | They build the shed only: no approvals, tenant, loan or operations. Their incentive is to sell *steel* |
| Brokers | IPCs (CBRE, JLL, C&W, Colliers, Knight Frank), local brokers | They bring a tenant | They don't build. Brokers become our **partners**, not competitors |
| Doing it yourself | Family + a contractor | "Save the fee" | Overruns of 15–30%, delays, and an empty shed. Our fee (3–4%) is smaller than one month of delay plus one bad contractor |

**Our position in one sentence:**
> For landowning families on Bangalore's warehouse belts who want rental income without selling or giving away half their land, we are the **open-book development partner** that finds the tenant, the money and the builders, and we earn only a disclosed fee.

## 3. Our three unfair advantages (use these everywhere)

1. **Tenant-first.** We line up a tenant LOI (or a very strong tenant pipeline) before the landowner spends heavily on construction. In a 15% vacancy market, this is *the* message.
2. **Open-book.** Every vendor quote is shown to the client, compared across a three-tier vendor matrix, and our fee is the only money we make. This is our strict "no hidden spread" rule, turned into a brand promise.
3. **We are landowners too.** The Bansal family builds on its own land (Hoskote 001). We are not consultants giving theory. We take the same risk ourselves, and we document every rupee publicly.

## 4. The offer ladder (fixes a contradiction in the original plan)

The original plan has the feasibility report as both a **paid** product (₹1.5–3 L) and a **free** lead magnet. Both are useful, so we split it in two:

| Step | Name | Price | What they get | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Land Potential Snapshot** | **Free** | 2-page PDF in 72 hours: buildable sq ft, rough cost band, rent band, indicative monthly income, the 3 biggest risks | Lead magnet for ads, website and meetings. Low effort for us (desk work from the RTC, Dishank map and a site photo) |
| 2 | **Site visit to Hoskote 001** | Free | See a real build, meet the team, see the numbers | Builds trust |
| 3 | **Detailed Feasibility Report** | ₹1.5 L (≤5 acres) / ₹2.25 L (5–10 acres) / ₹3 L (10+ acres); **100% credited** on signing the mandate | 15–20 page bankable report (see [`05-feasibility-report.md`](05-feasibility-report.md)) | First real commitment. Anyone who pays is serious |
| 4 | **Development Mandate** | 3–4% of construction cost + leasing + LRD fees | Full 7-stage delivery | Core revenue |
| 5 | **Asset Care** | Annual FM contract + optional 3–5% of rent collected for asset management | Operations, rent collection, renewals | Recurring revenue and referrals |

**New funnel (monthly targets):**
`30 conversations → 10 free Snapshots → 4 Hoskote site visits → 2 paid Feasibility Reports → 1 mandate (every 1–2 months)`

## 5. The two engines: why this is a real company, not a network business

Friends and family will give you the first one or two projects. They do not scale. We build **two engines** that produce leads without you personally knowing the person:

**Engine A — Ground engine (field + partners).** Kannada field BD, land brokers, village meetings, referral partners. This brings the highest-quality landowner leads and takes the most time. Expect 60–70% of Year-1 mandates from here.

**Engine B — Digital engine (ads + content + website).** Google Search ads catch people *already searching* ("lease my land for warehouse", "warehouse construction cost Bangalore"). Meta ads (Facebook/Instagram) and YouTube reach **the son or daughter in a Whitefield IT job whose family owns 6 acres in Hoskote**. That person is often the real decision-maker. LinkedIn reaches 3PL and manufacturer real-estate heads. Content (the Hoskote series) makes every other channel cheaper because people already trust us when they meet us.

Both engines feed **one CRM**, one funnel and one weekly review. That is how a company runs (see [`07-digital-marketing.md`](07-digital-marketing.md) §8).
