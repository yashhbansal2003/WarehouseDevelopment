# 04 — Scripts, Meeting Flow, Site Visit Flow and Objection Handling

> **Note for Yash: the three laws of sales conversations**
> 1. **Ask more than you tell.** Aim for the other person talking 60% of the time. Every script below has questions in it, so use them.
> 2. **One goal per conversation.** A first call is not for "selling a project". It is for getting the *next small step* (a Snapshot, a meeting, a site visit).
> 3. **Write down their exact words.** The phrases landowners use ("we don't want to sell", "my son decides") become your ad headlines.
>
> Practise each script aloud 10 times before using it. Record yourself on your phone. It feels awkward and it works.

---

## 1. Landowner: first contact

### 1A. Phone call via a referral (most common)

> **You:** Namaskara, am I speaking with **[Name]** avaru? … My name is Yash, from Sthira Developments. We're a Bansal Group company. **[Referrer name]** gave me your number and said you have land near **[village]**. Is this a good time for two minutes?
>
> *(If no → "When can I call back?" Fix a time. End.)*
>
> **You:** Thank you. I'll be brief. We help landowning families on the Hoskote and Nelamangala side earn **monthly rent from their land by building a warehouse on it**, without selling the land and without giving half of it away to a builder. We do everything: approvals, construction, the bank loan and finding the company that rents it.
>
> Can I ask a few quick questions about your land?
> 1. Roughly how many acres is it, and where exactly? Near which road?
> 2. Is it in your name, or is it family land shared with brothers or sisters?
> 3. What are you doing with it today?
> 4. Have you thought about what you want from it in the next 5–10 years: keep it, sell it, or earn from it?
>
> *(Listen. If it fits the ICP:)*
>
> **You:** Thank you, this sounds like it could work well. Here's what I'd suggest. Send me the survey number on WhatsApp, and within **3 days** we'll prepare a **free 2-page report**: how big a warehouse your land can take, roughly what it would cost, and what monthly rent it could earn. No obligation. Then, if you like, I'll show you our own warehouse that we're building in Hoskote, so you can see a real one.
>
> Shall I send you my WhatsApp now?

**Goal achieved = survey number on WhatsApp.**

### 1C. WhatsApp first message (inbound lead from ads / website)

> Namaskara **[Name]**, this is Yash from Sthira Developments (a Bansal Group company). Thank you for your enquiry about your land in **[area]**.
>
> To prepare your **free Land Potential Snapshot**, please send:
> 1️⃣ Survey number + village
> 2️⃣ Approximate acres
> 3️⃣ A Google Maps pin or photo of the approach road
>
> We'll send the report within 3 working days. You can also call me directly on this number. 🙏

*(Send the same in Kannada if they wrote in Kannada or if the ad was Kannada.)*

### 1D. WhatsApp follow-up sequence (if no reply)
- **Day 2:** "Namaskara, just checking that you saw my message. Many families near Hoskote are asking us what rent their land could earn. Happy to prepare yours."
- **Day 5:** Send a 45-second Hoskote 001 video + "This is our own warehouse under construction in Hoskote. Happy to show you in person."
- **Day 12:** "I won't disturb you further. Whenever you'd like the free report, just send the survey number. 🙏"
- Then add them to the monthly broadcast list (project updates only).

## 2. Broker: first contact

### 2A. Local land broker (phone)

> **You:** Namaskara **[Name]**, I'm Yash from Sthira Developments, a Bansal Group company. I saw your listings for land and warehouses around **[Hoskote]**. You clearly know the area well. Do you have two minutes?
>
> We develop warehouses on landowners' land: the owner keeps the land, and we build, lease and finance it. Many owners you meet **don't want to sell**, and you lose the deal. We'd like those owners.
>
> If you introduce a landowner who signs with us, **you earn a referral fee**: **10% of our fee, typically ₹4–10 lakh per project**: ₹25,000 within 15 days of the owner signing, and the rest as we get paid. You also stay the landowner's trusted person; we never cut you out.

> Do you have 2–3 owners in mind with 3 acres or more near the highway?

## 3. PEB company: first contact

> **Subject:** Two-way referral: warehouse projects in Bangalore
>
> Hi **[Name]**,
>
> I'm Yash from Sthira Developments (Bansal Group). We're a Bangalore warehouse development manager: we take landowners from land to a leased, financed warehouse.
>
> **What's in it for you:** we tender **[X] warehouse projects (50k–3L sq ft each)** over the next 18 months. We run a three-tier vendor matrix (premium / mid / cost-efficient) and would like **[Company]** on it for the Hoskote 001 tender and future projects.
>
> **What we'd ask:** your sales team meets landowners who ask, "Who will get me a tenant? Who will arrange the loan? Who will handle approvals?" Those are exactly the projects we manage, and the steel still goes out to tender, where you're on the list. We'd love to receive those introductions.
>
> Could we meet for 30 minutes? Happy to visit your office, or to show you our Hoskote site.

**Phone version, first 20 seconds:** *"I'm calling because we'll be putting several warehouse projects out to PEB tender in the next 18 months, and I'd like your company on our vendor list. I also think we can send each other business. Can I take 2 minutes to explain?"* (Leading with *their* gain gets you past the gatekeeper.)

## 4. Bank/NBFC LRD team: first contact

> **Subject:** Pipeline of leased warehouse LRD files, Bengaluru (₹10–40 Cr tickets)
>
> Dear **[Name]**,
>
> I'm Yash, founder of Sthira Developments (a Bansal Group company). We develop 50k–3L sq ft warehouses for landowners around Bengaluru and arrange construction finance and LRD for each project.
>
> **Current pipeline:**
> - Project 001, Hoskote: 1,13,000 sq ft PEB, construction ~₹13 Cr, LRD requirement ~₹[X] Cr after leasing. Revenue-share land lease, 20 years.
> - [N] further projects at feasibility stage, typical LRD ₹10–40 Cr.
>
> Every file comes with clean title reports, approved plans, fire NOC, a registered lease and a tenant credit profile. **We prepare the file the way your credit team wants it.**
>
> Could I understand your current appetite for warehouse LRD (ticket size, tenant profile, LTV, tenure, pricing) and what makes a file easy for you to sanction? 20 minutes at your office next week?

**Questions to ask in the meeting** (write the answers into the lender matrix):
1. Minimum and maximum ticket? 2. LTV: % of lease receivables or % of property value? 3. Do you fund a **revenue-share leasehold** structure like Hoskote (we don't own the land)? What do you need from the landowner (NOC, consent to mortgage the leasehold)? 4. Pricing for an MNC tenant vs an Indian 3PL? 5. Construction finance before leasing: available, and on what conditions? 6. Typical sanction timeline? 7. Escrow mechanics? 8. Do you pay any arranger/DSA fees? *(Ask the bank's **policy**. Never offer anything to an individual employee.)*

## 7. Landowner meeting (15–30 people), presentation flow

**Format:** 75 minutes in a Kalyana Mantapa, temple hall or community hall near the village. Kannada-led (field BD + you). Tea, snacks and a simple lunch afterwards. Invite through the local respected person (the "convenor") 7–10 days ahead, then remind by WhatsApp voice note 1 day before. **Bring:** printed Kannada brochure, a large printed map of the area with the highways and industrial zones, a projector or a 55" TV, Snapshot request forms, a registration sheet (name, phone, survey no., acres).

| Min | Section | What happens | Slide / prop |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Welcome | Convenor introduces you. You thank the elders by name | — |
| 5–12 | **"Your land is sitting next to a highway that is changing"** | Map: NH-75/NH-48/STRR, Amazon's 2.78 L sq ft lease in Nelamangala, DHL's 2.4 L sq ft in Hoskote, the new airport-side demand. "Companies are paying ₹20–26 per sq ft *per month*" | Map + 2 news headlines in Kannada |
| 12–20 | **"Three choices for your land"** | (1) Sell: one lump sum, land gone. (2) Give to a JDA builder: 40–60% gone forever. (3) **Develop and keep**: monthly rent for 20–30 years, land stays yours. Give honest pros and cons for each | 3-column slide |
| 20–32 | **"What one acre can earn"** | Simple maths in Kannada: 1 acre ≈ 22,000–24,000 sq ft of warehouse → at ₹21/sq ft ≈ **₹4.6–5 lakh rent per month** (before loan EMI and costs). Then show the honest net figure after loan repayment | Big-number slide |
| 32–42 | **"Our own warehouse in Hoskote"** | 3-minute Hoskote 001 video. "This is the Bansal family's own land arrangement. We're doing it ourselves first" | Video |
| 42–50 | **"How it works: seven steps, one team"** | Feasibility → approvals → design → construction → loan → tenant → maintenance. Emphasise: **tenant first; open book; you see every quote; the land stays in your name** | 7-step graphic |
| 50–65 | **Questions** | Let elders ask first. Note every question; they become FAQ, ads and content | — |
| 65–75 | **Next step** | "Anyone who wants a **free report** on their land, give us your survey number today. Next **[Saturday]** we'll take a bus to our Hoskote site." Collect forms. Group photo (with permission) | Forms |

**After the meeting (within 48 hours):** WhatsApp thank-you with a photo, then Snapshots delivered within 3–7 days, then the site-visit invite.

**Rules:** never promise a rent figure as guaranteed. Never criticise a named local builder. Never discuss any individual's land in front of others.

## 8. Hoskote 001 site-visit flow (90 minutes)

**Purpose:** convert "interested" into "I trust these people". Do it on a **fixed day** (e.g., every 2nd and 4th Saturday, 10:30 am) so it's routine, and add individual visits for hot leads.

| Time | Station | What they see / hear | Who leads |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | **Arrival** at a branded gate with a site hoarding | Safety helmets with the logo; welcome by name; water/juice | Field BD |
| 0:05 | **Site office**: "the war room" | Wall with the project timeline (plan vs actual), the approvals file (land conversion order, plan approval, fire NOC), the vendor-matrix board showing three quotes per item | You |
| 0:20 | **Walk the building** | Foundations/columns, roof, flooring, docks, fire system. Project head explains each in simple words: *why* FM2 floor, *why* dock height 1.2 m, *why* 12 m clear height | Project head |
| 0:45 | **"Who rents this"** | Leasing head: the tenant profile, rent, lease length, how tenants choose buildings | Leasing/BD head |
| 0:55 | **"Your land"** (one family at a time) | Walk through their Snapshot at the site-office table; answer questions; show the 3 budget tiers | You |
| 1:15 | **Next step** | Offer the paid Feasibility Report (credited on mandate); give a printed sample report; agree a date | You |
| 1:25 | **Tea + photo** | With permission, a photo for the "visitors" wall | — |

**Bring home:** a printed Snapshot, the brochure, a sample feasibility report, and a small gift (a branded steel water bottle or a sweet box).

**KPI:** ≥ 50% of site visits should buy the paid Feasibility Report within 30 days.

## 9. Objection handling

> **Method (LAER):** **L**isten fully → **A**cknowledge ("That's a fair concern") → **E**xplore ("Can I ask what makes you feel that?") → **R**espond with proof, not opinion.

### Landowners

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| **"I don't want to lose my land."** | "Exactly right, and you won't. In our model the land stays in your name. You give a long-term lease, registered, with clear clauses on what happens at the end. After 20–30 years the building comes back to you too. Shall I show you the clause?" |
| **"Builders cheat. How do I trust you?"** | "You shouldn't trust any of us on words. That's why we're open-book: you'll see every contractor's quote, three per item, and choose. Our fee is fixed in writing, and it's our *only* income from your project. And come see Hoskote, where we're doing it with our own family's land." |
| **"Your fee is too high." / "Why pay 3–4%?"** | "On a ₹20 Cr build, our fee is about ₹70 L. A typical self-managed project runs 15–20% over budget, which is ₹3–4 Cr, and 6 months late, which is about ₹1.5 Cr of lost rent. We're paid to prevent both. We also tie part of our fee to handover, so we only earn it when you get the building." |
| **"I don't have ₹15–20 Cr."** | "Most families don't. There are three routes: (1) a bank construction loan, repaid from the rent; (2) a revenue-share model like our Hoskote project, where we or an investor build and you earn a share of rent with zero investment; (3) start with phase 1 on part of the land. The feasibility report tells you which fits." |
| **"What if no tenant comes? I heard there's a lot of empty warehouses."** | "You've heard right. Vacancy in Bangalore is 11–15%. That's why our rule is **tenant first**: we don't advise heavy construction spend without a signed LOI or a very strong tenant pipeline. Plain sheds built speculatively sit empty; built-to-suit buildings don't." |
| **"My neighbour got a lump-sum offer of ₹X Cr per acre."** | "Selling is a fair option. Let's compare honestly: the lump sum, versus 20–30 years of rent with the land still yours. The feasibility report puts both on one page. If selling is better for your family, we'll tell you, and even introduce serious buyers." |
| **"My son/brother needs to decide."** | "Of course. This is a family decision. Shall we do a short video call with them this week, or can they join the Hoskote visit on Saturday?" *(Always get all decision-makers into one meeting.)* |
| **"Let me think about it."** | "Of course. To help you think, may I ask: is it the money, the trust, or the timing that you want to think about?" *(Then answer that specific thing. Fix the next date before leaving.)* |
| **"Why is the feasibility report paid?"** | "The free Snapshot is a quick estimate. The detailed report needs our engineer, a legal title review and real vendor quotes. It's what a bank uses. That's why it costs ₹1.5–3 L, and **it's fully adjusted in our fee** if you go ahead." |
| **"Approvals take forever."** | "They can. Our approvals consultant does this every month, and since the 2025 amendments, conversion paperwork in Karnataka is more standardised. We'll give you a written approvals timeline in the feasibility report and update you every week." |

### Occupiers

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "There's a lot of ready vacant space; why BTS?" | "Absolutely. For a standard need, take ready space. We're for when you need a location, height, floor or dock layout the market doesn't have, or a size the parks can't carve out. Let's check what's ready first. If nothing fits, we build." |
| "You're new; what's your track record?" | "Fair. Hoskote 001 is live. Come and see the build quality. Our project head has [12–15] years in PEB and civil. And our contracts have delivery penalties, so the risk of delay sits with us and the contractor." |
| "7–9 months is too slow." | "If you can sign an LOI on a pre-approved site, PEB fabrication runs in parallel with approvals. The critical path is usually the approvals, and we start with sites where conversion is already done." |
| "Rent is higher than X park." | "Let's compare like for like: specs, location (your trucking cost), lock-in and fit-out. Often a building 8 km closer to your client saves more in trucking than the rent difference." |

### Brokers / partners

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "You'll go around me to my client." | "It's in writing: protected introductions for 18 months, and fees paid within 30 days of us getting paid. Our model needs brokers; cutting you out would kill our pipeline." |
| "Why not just sell this land to a big developer?" | "If they'll buy, great, and we'll pay you for the introduction even then. But most families here won't sell, and those deals die. We turn 'won't sell' into a fee for you." |
